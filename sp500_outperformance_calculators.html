Probability of Outperforming the S&P 500

Interactive illustrations of how portfolio size and correlation affect the probability of outperformance under a joint normal model of individual stocks' excess returns.

1. Independent-stock probability calculator

Assume equally weighted stocks with independent, identically distributed normal excess returns. Adjust the proportion of individual stocks that outperform and the number held.

Probability portfolio outperforms—
P(outperform) = Φ(√n × Φ⁻¹(p))

p = individual stock probability of outperforming; n = equal-weight portfolio size. The curve updates with p; the highlighted point shows your selected n.

2. How pairwise correlations change probability

Assume all stocks have the same marginal normal excess-return distribution and the same pairwise excess-return correlation. Adjust the values to see how correlated stock performance changes portfolio outcomes.

Probability portfolio outperforms at selected correlation—
Pairwise excess-return correlationPortfolio outperformance probability
P(outperform) = Φ(Φ⁻¹(p) / √[ρ + (1 − ρ)/n])

This model assumes a common correlation for every pair. It is not an estimate of the actual S&P 500 covariance matrix, and it uses correlation of stock returns relative to the index, not correlation of raw stock returns.

Educational model, not an empirical forecast. Real stock excess returns are skewed, heteroskedastic, and have heterogeneous correlations. Also, the share of stocks outperforming alone does not determine portfolio outperformance without distributional assumptions.
